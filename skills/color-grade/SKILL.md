# Colour grade

Grade after picture lock, before captions. The loop: **probe → measure → candidates → check → back off**.
Numbers are readings with their source, not targets: what to change and how much follows the footage and the
intent. Every `command_run` is one undo step (`edit.undo`), so a bad try costs nothing.

## BashCutPro's colour commands

| Job | Command |
|---|---|
| Read a frame as numbers | `scopes.read {scopes: ["waveform", "parade", "histogram", "vectorscopeYuv"], seconds}` (the timeline composite at that time) |
| Look at a frame | MCP `render_frame {seconds}`; a source file: `file.exportFrame {target: "source", item, time, path}` |
| Source colour facts | `media.colorInfo {item}` (transfer, primaries, range), `color.spaces` |
| A LUT into the project | `lut.import {path, name}` → id `lib:<id>`; `lut.list` also lists `builtin:<id>` looks |
| Look on a clip | `lumetri.setLook {clip, lut}` (creative look), `lumetri.setInputLut {clip, lut}` (technical: log or HLG to Rec.709 first) |
| Presets | `lumetri.presets`, `lumetri.presetThumbnails {names, path}` (one sheet), `lumetri.applyPreset {name, clips}` |
| Match one shot to another | `lumetri.applyMatch {clip, referenceTime}` (faces protected by default) |
| One grade over the edit | `file.newAdjustmentLayer {seconds}`, `timeline.place {item, track: "new", time: 0, duration}`, then `lumetri.setLook` on the placed clip |
| Strength, sections | `effects.setParam` on the clip's Lumetri Color effect; `lumetri.setSection {clip, section, on}` |
| Before / after | `effects.toggleEnabled {clip, index}` on the Lumetri effect, read, toggle back |
| Keep a look | `presets.save {clip, name}`, `lut.export {lut, path}` |

Durations in params are ticks (254 016 000 000 per second) unless a param says `seconds`. Find clip ids and the
Lumetri effect's index with `sequence_inspect`; read a parameter's exact name there before `effects.setParam`
(never guess it). Grade picture tracks only: an adjustment layer grades every video track below it, so keep
titles and captions above it.

## 1. Probe

`media.colorInfo` per source. A transfer other than SDR Rec.709 (iPhones shoot HLG by default; `log` is flat until
converted) needs a technical conversion first: `lumetri.setInputLut` with the camera maker's log-to-709 LUT
(`lut.import`), or tell the user which clips are HLG / PQ / log instead of grading over them. The looks in
`looks.json` were fitted on Rec.709.

## 2. Measure

`scopes.read` at several times per clip (`sequence_inspect` gives each clip's range), not one frame. Find the
**brightest, darkest and skin-heavy** clips: they are the check frames later. Read the scale from the result
(waveform columns, histogram bins); the ranges below are on 0–100.

| Reading | Range seen | What moves it |
|---|---|---|
| Mid luma, daylight vlog | ~30–50 | lower on purpose for night, low-key, silhouettes: not "too dark" |
| Clipped highlights | share near 0 | deliberate sun or sky may clip |
| Matte blacks / rolled whites | black > 3 reads as matte; white < 90 as rolled | examples, not targets |
| Saturation | < 30 reads muted, > 45 punchy | genre: food punchy, cinematic muted |
| Split tone | shadow R−B < 0 with highlight R−B > 0 = teal-orange | parade per band |
| Shot match | each clip's difference from the median clip | you choose how far to pull an outlier |

The brief's intent ("bright, warm, skies kept") becomes which readings matter, never fixed numbers. On source
files `grade.py measure` gives the same readings from ffmpeg frames (black, p5, mid, p95, white, saturation, tint
per band; see REFERENCE.md).

## 3. Candidates

**One sheet, one pick.** Turn the intent into a target, then show few candidates on the check frames:

1. Presets: `lumetri.presets`, then `lumetri.presetThumbnails {names: [...], path}` draws the chosen ones on the
   current frame in one image; read it.
2. LUTs: `lut.list` (built-in and imported). The user's own `.cube` files: `lut.import`.
3. A generated look when nothing is close: `grade.py lut` writes a `.cube` from a look in `looks.json`, fitted with
   `match` on the source (REFERENCE.md), then `lut.import`.

| Look (`looks.json`) | Use for | Fitted reference |
|---|---|---|
| `matte-cinematic` | default cinematic: street, daytime, travel | black ~7, white ~84, sat ~28; teal shadows, peach highlights, greens to olive, skin protected |
| `warm-film` | warm indoor light, late afternoon, personal stories | deep blacks (~2.6), highlights ~66–76, sat ~29, warm mids |
| `faded-memory` | hook, flashback or emotional low point, then back to colour | near black-and-white, lifted blacks |
| `teal-orange`, `clean-bright`, `moody-green`, `bw`, `film-print` | blockbuster / travel; food, product, tutorial; forest, rain, night; drama; narrative | set by hand, not fitted (`grade.py looks`) |

Apply the pick once over the edit: adjustment layer as long as the sequence, placed on a new top video track,
`lumetri.setLook {clip: <adjustment clip>, lut: "lib:<id>"}`. One clip only: `lumetri.setLook` on that clip.

- **Strength** 0.5–1.0; 0.6–0.8 is typical (openmontage). Lower for log-like or already saturated footage,
  skin-heavy shots and mixed light.
- **One look for the video**; fix outliers per clip (`lumetri.applyMatch` to a good clip, or that clip's own
  Lumetri) rather than a second global look.
- A LUT is half the look, light when shooting the other half. Harsh noon sun stays harsh: say so instead of
  pushing the grade until skin breaks.
- A matte look lifts pure black backgrounds to dark grey; keep it off those ranges if true black matters.

## 4. Check

On each check frame (brightest, darkest, skin-heavy): `scopes.read` with the look on, toggle the Lumetri effect
off (`effects.toggleEnabled`), `scopes.read` again, toggle it back. Then `render_frame` the graded frames and look,
once at phone size (`max_side` ~400).

Read damage **relative to the source**. One worked example (DaVinci Resolve's grade checker, thresholds are
parameters): clipped pixels grown by 0.5 %, washed-out chroma ratio 0.60, milky black lift L* 8; and a grade that
moved a lot without any flag "is worth a second opinion": undamaged is not the same as good. grade.py's defaults
drop a candidate past 1 % more clipped highlights (0.5–2 seen), 2 % more crushed blacks (1–3) or a skin hue moved
by 8° (5–12). A matte look lifts blacks on purpose; a warm look moves the mid tint on purpose: compare with the
intent. Check every sampled clip, not the average; name the clip and time that fails.

## 5. Back off

On damage, lower before adding: the look's strength first, then the exposure or white balance you gave
`grade.py`. Resolve backs off ×0.8 per try down to 0.5. Change the parameter (`effects.setParam`) or `edit.undo`
and apply again. **At most 3 attempts.** Then stop: show the user `render_frame` of the gentlest attempt with its
numbers and ask.

## Keep what worked

Once the user approves: `presets.save {clip: <adjustment clip>, name}` keeps the Lumetri settings as an effect
preset; `lut.export` writes the look as a `.cube` for other projects. Matching a reference channel's look:
`bcp:style-study` measures its frames; move a look's params toward them.

`grade.py candidates` (and `fetch_luts.py`) needed BashCut's library and its Online Resources plugin; they are not
part of this kit. The other `grade.py` commands run as they did.
